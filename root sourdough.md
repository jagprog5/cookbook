# Root Sourdough

This recipe is for an old-style, firm, healthy, and nutty sourdough bread which
is low on flour and high in gluten.

This makes a small loaf; consider doubling the recipe.

## Baked Sweet Potato

Place 1 medium-large sized purple japanese sweet yam on parchment paper on a
baking sheet.

Or, substitute with any vegetable which can be baked then easily mashed. It
should be moist. For example:

- any sweet potato or yam
- squash (buttercup, butternut, ...)
- absolutely *DO NOT* use normal potatoes

Bake the yam at 400F for 1hr. After baking, the skin can be pulled off and
discarded. Let it cool to room temperature before mixing with other ingredients.
Otherwise the heat will kill the yeast starter.

The final weight of this ingredient should be 200g. Add it to a mixing bowl.

## Bread Components

Add 20g of sourdough starter to the mixing bowl. Replenish the starter by adding
10g of water and 10g of any wheat-based flour into the starter container (a 1:1
ratio) and stir.

Starter is generally kept in the fridge for weekly baking, or at room
temperature daily baking. More info can be found online for maintaining a
starter.

Next add 50g whole wheat flour and 40g gluten flour into the mixing bowl.

## Assorted Nuts & Seeds

Add to the mixing bowl:
- 30g crushed walnut
- 20g sunflower seeds
- 10g flax seeds

## Mix

With effort, mix the contents of the mixing bowl.

While initially daunting, the water content of the baked yam is sufficient to
allow the ingredients to mix into a dough. Only if desperate, add water or
cashew milk (but not almond milk) sparingly to assist with conglomeration.

Consider squishing the baked yam repeatedly with a wooden spoon or by hand. Each
squish exposes wet content to absorb the ingredients.

## Delay

Place the dough on parchment paper in a loaf pan.

Let the dough ferment for at least 12 hours at room temperature in a sheltered
area; for example, an oven that is turned off.

## Bake

Bake uncovered at 400F for 1 hr. If needed, bake for longer; it is difficult to
burn this recipe due to the root vegetable content - it instead caramelizes into
a nice crust.

If the bread is made daily, consider baking the yam for tomorrow at the same
time as the loaf for today.

Leave the bread in the oven after baking and only take it out when convenient.
Consider using the automatic start and stop time functionality found in modern
ovens. For example, the bread can start baking over night and cool somewhat
before breakfast.
